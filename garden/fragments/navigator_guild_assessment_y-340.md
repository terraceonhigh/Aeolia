# Navigator Guild — Route Certification Assessment
## Candidate: Yela Ouen-Kast, third passage, outer-approach grade
## Examining Pilot: Senior Route-Holder Nai Marek
## Location: Outer survey position, northern Trade Circuit approach
## Assessment type: Practical (hull-lying; counter-swell reading; star bearing verification)

---

**Examiner's record:**

Candidate arrived at survey position at first light. Wind: light from the northeast, consistent with late Trade Circuit season. Swell: primary from the north-northwest at approximately nine feet, secondary cross-swell from the east at four feet. Sky: clear.

I gave the candidate no information about prevailing conditions. I asked her to read the water.

She went into the hull immediately, which is the correct first move. She lay there for longer than a senior route-holder would need — approximately twelve minutes — which is appropriate for a candidate at this level. When she came up she reported: primary swell north-northwest, secondary swell east. She further reported a counter-swell component running from the west-southwest, which she estimated at approximately two feet, originating below the primary swell.

I confirmed the counter-swell. I asked her what it indicated.

She said: land, or a shelf rising to meet the surface, at the bearing of the counter-swell's origin. West-southwest of our position. Probably within sixty miles, possibly less if the counter-swell is reflecting rather than propagating.

I asked her how certain she was.

She said she was certain about the counter-swell. She was less certain about the distance. She said she would want to run toward the bearing for half a day before committing to a distance estimate.

This is the correct answer. An incorrect answer would have been a distance estimate delivered with confidence. The swell tells you direction before it tells you distance, and it only tells you distance after you have read it from close enough to see the cloud-fix. She knows this. She did not feel the need to appear to know more than she does.

---

**Star bearing assessment:**

At dusk I asked her to set our position using stars only — no instruments. She used the star she uses for the northern Trade Circuit approach (she did not use the instrument name; she used the star name in the form she was taught it, which is not the cartographic designation). Her position fix was within acceptable error for a candidate at this level.

I asked her what she would do if the sky were covered.

She said she would go into the hull and hold the swell bearing. She said she would slow the vessel and increase time between heading checks. She said she would not run at full speed in unfamiliar water at night with covered sky, because the counter-swell signature for a reef approach is not sufficiently distinct from the open-water counter-swell at speed.

This is correct. I have known senior route-holders who did not know this. I have known senior route-holders who were therefore dead.

---

**Land-smell assessment:**

On the second morning I brought us to within estimated thirty miles of the shelf approach without telling the candidate our position. I asked her what she could tell me.

She said: land. She said she could smell it. She described it correctly — not the smell of seaweed, which is a coastal smell that means you are too close, but the smell of soil and vegetation carried on the offshore night wind, which means you are at distance. She estimated thirty to forty miles, which was consistent with our actual position.

I asked her what she would do next.

She said she would take a cloud-fix at dawn. She said she would wait for the cloud to be fully established — not just any cloud, but the cloud that does not move with the wind because there is land under it holding it in place.

I did not show her the cloud. She found it herself at first light, at the correct bearing, without instruction.

---

**Recommendation:**

Certified. Outer-approach grade.

There are fourteen active route-holders in the guild registry. Yela Ouen-Kast will be the fifteenth. She is ready to apprentice for the approaches she does not yet know. She is not ready to certify new approaches without a senior route-holder present. She knows this. She told me so without being asked.

The distinction between knowing a routing and knowing what you do not know is the only distinction that matters for this work. She has the first. She also has the second, which is rarer.
