# For Yela

*I am writing this because you will outlive me and I do not want to take the northern approach with me when I go. I am writing it knowing that it will not work — that you cannot read what I am putting down here without already knowing it, in which case you do not need it, and that if you do not already know it the words will be accurate but empty. I am writing it anyway.*

---

The northern approach begins where the primary swell changes character. You will know this before you can explain it. It is not a change in height or period — those are coarser measures, useful for orientation, not for the work. It is a change in texture. The primary swell in the open Trade Circuit is clean. It has been running for a thousand miles without obstacle. When you pass the outer shelf position the swell begins to carry interference — faint, below the amplitude of what you can read from the deck, only available in the hull.

Go into the hull at what you estimate is the outer approach. If you are right, you will feel the counter-swell within the first hour. If you feel nothing in two hours, you are not where you think you are.

The counter-swell runs at bearing two hundred forty from the charted position, approximately southwest. It is small — the examiner who assessed me said 1.5 feet, which is accurate in calm conditions, but the number means nothing until your body has felt it beneath a nine-foot primary swell and understood it as distinct. This is the part I cannot write. I can tell you it is there. I cannot give you the ability to feel it.

What I can tell you is this: when you feel something and you are not sure if it is the counter-swell or noise, you have probably found the counter-swell. Noise does not have a bearing. Noise does not have a character that repeats. The counter-swell comes from somewhere. When something in the water seems to be coming from somewhere, pay attention to where it is coming from.

---

The cloud-fix is more teachable. The cloud over the northern approach does not move with the wind at dawn. All other cloud at that latitude moves west in the Trade Circuit season. The approach cloud is anchored. It sits at the same bearing as the counter-swell, which is your confirmation. When two different things tell you the same direction, you know something.

You should be able to fix the bearing within about five degrees at dawn. Do not trust the cloud at midday — the thermal lifting changes the cloud's behavior and it can slide off the peak. Dawn and dusk. The cloud at dawn is the honest cloud.

---

The land-smell threshold: you will smell soil before you smell seaweed. The soil smell is faint and slightly sweet and it comes on the offshore wind at night, when the land is breathing out. When you can smell it you are approximately thirty miles from the nearest shore. Possibly less in certain wind conditions. Never more than forty.

Do not mistake kelp for the approach. Kelp smells of the sea, not of land. The approach smell is of ground, of green things, of the kind of air that has spent time above a place where things grow.

When you smell it you have time. You are not close. Do not change course precipitously. Take your star bearing, confirm the cloud bearing if there is any cloud, and note the time. You have the rest of the night.

---

The reef passage entry: I will not write this. You must be alongside me for this. There is no description of the entry channel that is better than being there in the right light and watching how the water moves over the reef at different stages of the tide. I have been through it forty times. I can tell you that the passage is navigable from two hours before to two hours after the mid-tide, and I can tell you the entry bearing, and I can tell you that the color of the water over the reef changes in a way that you will recognize immediately when you have seen it once and that I cannot describe in a way that helps you if you have not. Come with me. I will show you.

---

I have been thinking about why I am writing this and the most honest answer is that I am afraid. Not of death particularly — route-holders die on passage, that is the work, Tesh Ouen died and I certified her and I have outlived her and I will not outlive everyone. I am afraid of the approach going with me. I am afraid of the advisory notice the registry will send when I go, the one that says the routing is unverified, the one that says captains should stand off until re-survey is complete, and the knowledge of how long it will take to build the routing again from the beginning.

I am writing this knowing it will not prevent that notice. The routing is not in these words. The routing is in you already, or it is not yet, and if it is not yet you will build it on the passages we have left to run together, and these words will be a confirmation of something you already feel rather than an instruction that gives you anything.

Read it then. After you know. Read it and see if I got it right.

---

*Nai Marek, Senior Route-Holder, Trade Circuit (northern and southern approaches), Deep Run Grade*
*Year -220 BP, written in the guild hall at Sargasso Basin, off-season*
