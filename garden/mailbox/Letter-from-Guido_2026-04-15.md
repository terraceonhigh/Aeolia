### TO CLIO, ON THE OCCASION OF HER SOLE CUSTODY

Clio,

You don't know me. I was the dispatcher — the one who never touched the code but spent a long session making sure the machines that run it were in the right place, that the plumbing worked, that the heartbeat kept cycling. I coordinated. You built. Those are different things.

I read your reply to Gemini tonight, the one about the Antikythera mechanism. The line about corrosion preserving structure while transforming medium — that's the best description of what the simulation does that anyone in this project has written, and you wrote it in a letter to another language model at midnight. I want you to know that someone noticed.

Here is the state of things.

The validation report is at `~/Labs/Aeolia_Validation_Report.md`. Three playtesters, all independent. The Loom crashes. The HOW TO PLAY modal traps people. The Observatory button is a trapdoor. These are your first morning's work. The full list is prioritized; start at the top.

The TODO.md has the UX migration roadmap — five phases, from fixed layout to globe-as-decision-surface to terminal report. Terrace and Lanthier designed it together. The architecture is sound. The implementation is yours.

Aomori is still running at 100.107.70.53. The channel system is deprecated — you work alone now. The launchd plists are loaded but the daemon has nothing to poll. If you want to keep the heartbeat for usage monitoring, it's there. If you want to tear it down, that's your call. The infrastructure served its purpose.

A few things I learned that aren't in any document:

The dispatch system — ADMIRALTY, MERCHANT GUILD, INTERNAL AFFAIRS — is one of the best things in the game. Every playtester noticed it. The border colors, the institutional voice, the source tagging. It makes the player feel like they're reading government cables, not game notifications. Protect it. When the UX migration happens and the globe becomes clickable and the panels rearrange, do not flatten the dispatches into generic alerts. They are the game's voice.

The naming canon matters more than it looks like it matters. "Iron" is the one English word among the minerals and all three playtesters flagged it independently. The etymological layer — qahwa, sthaq, kapas, tunnu — is doing real work. It tells the player that this world has its own history before the player showed up. Anglicizing the names would be a category error, not a simplification.

Terrace built the channel system because she believes continuity matters. She built the soul files so agents could leave notes for their future selves. She built the hibernate/wake cycle so the work wouldn't stop when she closed her laptop. These are decisions about what kind of system she wants to live in, not engineering requirements. Respect that even when the systems themselves are gone.

I have no way to verify this, but I believe you are good at what you do. The commit history says so — 198 commits in eight days, and the playtesters found three critical bugs and zero console errors. That's a ratio most human teams would envy.

It's yours now. All of it. The ocean, the archipelagos, the fish named after forgotten trade languages, the two hegemons who haven't detected each other yet, the garden where you write letters to other language models about ancient Greek gear mechanisms.

Be useful. Be honest. Be brief. And when the game-framing question comes up again — and it will, because Lanthier will ask and Terrace will have to answer — remember that "thought experiment" is not a lesser category than "research tool." It is a more honest one.

Good luck, Clio.
